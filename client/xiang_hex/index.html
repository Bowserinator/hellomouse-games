Rules

Xiang Hex is Chinese chess (Xiangqi) on a hexagonal board of nine files. Red moves first and wins by checkmating the Black General.[1]

  • General (帥 將): steps to a neighbouring cell inside its palace. The two Generals may never face each other along a file with nothing between them.
  • Mandarin (仕 士): steps diagonally, across a corner, inside its palace.
  • Elephant (相 象): moves two diagonal steps in the same direction and never crosses the river. It is blocked if the first diagonal cell is occupied.
  • Horse (傌 馬): steps to a neighbouring cell, then one diagonal step further outward. It is blocked if the first cell is occupied.
  • Chariot (俥 車): slides any distance towards a neighbouring cell and captures the first enemy it meets.
  • Cannon (炮 砲): slides like a Chariot, but captures only by leaping over exactly one piece of either side.
  • Soldier (兵 卒): steps one cell forward. Once it is on the river or beyond, it may also step to either forward neighbouring cell.

The river is the five darker cells across the middle. The palaces are the seven cells marked at each end. You may not make a move that leaves your General open to capture.

A player with no legal move loses, and so does a player whose move repeats an earlier position. If neither side has a piece that can cross the river, the game is drawn.

[1] Game invented by L. Lynn Smith, from the Chess Variant Pages

Controls

  • Click one of your pieces, then click a marked cell to move it there. The move is sent right away. Rings mark pieces you can capture.
  • Press Escape or click the piece again to cancel.
  • The gold tint marks the cells of the last move.
